Lesson 5 · 20 min

LOOP-R: the process that learns

Hora handles nine hundred conversations a week and, the next week, works the same way. Every run generates evidence that nobody uses. LOOP-R closes the cycle: the run becomes evidence, the evidence becomes a hypothesis, the hypothesis becomes a test, and only what is proven goes into the next version. In this lesson you run a full cycle on Hora, from running to promoting.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The seven steps of the cycle and who does each one
  2. 2Separate what is strong from what is noise in the evidence
  3. 3Write the hypothesis with a metric, guardrail and tolerance
  4. 4Test against the current version and decide with a five-line card

Goal: run a complete LOOP-R cycle, from executing to promoting, with a hypothesis, a test against the baseline and a decision from someone who did not propose it.

02 · The four decisions of the cycle

The cycle has seven steps, but four of them call for a manager's decision.

1. Strong, moderate or weak

Not every observation deserves a test. The LOOP-R ruler: with 90 cases or more, the evidence is strong and becomes a priority hypothesis; from 30 to 89, moderate; below 30, weak, and it goes to an "observed, not tested" list until more cases pile up.

A one-off complaint opens a question, not a change. That is the rule of the method: feedback does not change the agent in production.

At Horizonte640 delivery-time conversations: strong. "Tuesday sells more," with 14 orders: weak, goes to the list.

2. Hypothesis with a guardrail

Every hypothesis states the change, the metric that should improve, why, and what must not get worse, with the tolerance written down beforehand. Zero tolerance on a rare event throws out good ideas by chance.

LOOP-R calculates the tolerance as two standard errors of today's rate in the planned sample. For missed promises, 1.2% in 330 conversations, that comes to 1.2 points: the limit is 2.4%.

VetoAnother idea, "offer free shipping to close the deal," was vetoed before the test: it touches price, which is an invariant of the policy.

3. The sample size that proves it

Before testing, work out how many cases the test needs. With the two-proportion formula that LOOP-R uses (95% confidence, 80% power), seeing a drop from 45% to 25% takes about 89 delivery-time conversations in each version.

With less than the minimum, the answer is "insufficient sample," even if B looks like it's winning. Stopping early because it's winning is the most common way to promote noise.

At HorizonteTwo-week pilot: 330 delivery-time conversations on A and 326 on B, well above 89.

4. Who decides, and with what

Whoever proposed it does not approve it. The decision comes on a five-line card: hypothesis, result with N, what was watched, cost and the three options (approve, reject, wait for more data). With no answer by the deadline, the default is not to promote.

In LOOP-R, the five letters sum up the cycle for anyone just starting: Localizar, Operar, Observar, Propor and Reforçar. They are the same steps, in fewer words.

Simulation: illustrative example

Cycle no. 0001 · Hora v2.1 → v2.2 · Horizonte, fictional study company · relative weeks

  1. Run: v2.1, weeks 1 to 4, 3,600 conversations.
  2. Observe: 640 delivery-time or route conversations; Juliana took over 288 (45.0%). In the 30 cases, 2 of the 4 incorrect ones are about delivery time.
  3. Critique: strong strength (N = 640). Probable cause: the route and days-per-city table is missing.
  4. Propose: IF the table (owner: Valdir, logistics) goes into the package, THEN taken over from 45% to 25% or less. Without worsening: broken promises (tolerance 1.2 points), critical 0, AI up to R$ 0.35 per conversation. Vetoed: free shipping.
  5. Try: 30 cases on v2.2: 24 correct, 4 acceptable, 2 incorrect, 0 critical, no regression. Pilot in weeks 5 and 6, even days v2.1 and odd days v2.2; minimum of 89 per version.
  6. Validate: taken over A 148/330 (44.8%) × B 59/326 (18.1%); broken promises A 4/330 (1.2%) × B 5/326 (1.5%), limit 2.4%; critical 0 and 0. Verdict: B won.
  7. Promote: proposal by Patrícia (observation by Juliana); Carlos approves in week 7. v2.1 is kept.

The LOOP-R repository has four cycles run by the assistants on synthetic data (an aesthetics clinic, proposals over WhatsApp). The example itself says what is real and what is simulated.

  • 0001: the first idea was vetoed by the guardian; the second was designed.
  • 0002: response from 19.3% to 29.7%, but complaints 6 against 1 with zero tolerance: "A stays." The lesson was to fix the tolerance for a rare event.
  • 0003: new hypothesis designed; another vetoed.
  • 0004: response from 19.3% to 29.3%, guardrails within limits: promoted to v2. Conversion did not move: the link between the signal and the money is still unproven.
  • Changing because of a complaint. One case opens a question; change only after the test.
  • Hypothesis without a guardrail. Improving what you measure by worsening what you don't.
  • Tolerance decided afterward. Written after the result, it becomes a justification.
  • Stopping when it's winning. Before the minimum, the answer is "insufficient sample."
  • Whoever proposed it approves. Whoever proposes believes in the idea; whoever decides needs to look at the card.

05 · Practice

Hora's cycle with this lesson's numbers; your own cycle, on a process of yours.

1 · Hora's cycle 0001

30 min

Without looking at the example tab, fill in the cycle log with this lesson's Horizonte numbers, across the seven steps. Run the critic prompt on your hypothesis and answer the alternative explanation it points out. Finish with the five-line card.

Done when: the log has all 7 steps filled in; the hypothesis has a change, a metric with a starting value and a target, a reason, and a guardrail with a tolerance; the minimum per version is written with the math; the verdict is one of the three answers; and the card names who decides, someone other than who proposed it.

2 · A cycle on your own process

2 h in total

Use the lesson 4 panel to find the problem with strong or moderate evidence. Write the hypothesis with a guardrail, run the candidate on your 30 cases, run the pilot with the sample size that proves it, and take the card to whoever decides. If the sample doesn't reach the minimum, record "insufficient sample" and what to do next.

Done when: your cycle log has all 7 steps with numbers and N; the candidate passed the 30 cases with no critical; the verdict was given by someone who didn't propose; and the decision (approve, reject or wait) is recorded with a date.

07 · Summary

Seven stepsDo, observe, critique, propose, experiment, validate, promote. And repeat.
Strength of evidenceStrong at 90 or more; weak below 30 goes on the watch list.
Complete hypothesisChange, metric, why, and a guardrail with the tolerance written beforehand.
One of threeB won, A stays, or insufficient sample. Whoever did not propose it decides.
