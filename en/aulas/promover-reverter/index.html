Lesson 6 · 20 min

Promote and roll back

Hora v2.2 won the test and was promoted. A week later, logistics changed the delivery day for one route and the agent's table went stale. The conversations handled by Juliana stayed low, but missed delivery-time promises went over the limit. Promoting and rolling back are planned steps: in this lesson you write both before you need them.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What comes with a promotion: phases, watch, and a trigger to go back
  2. 2Calculate the trigger so you don't roll back by chance
  3. 3Roll back fast, warning first and keeping the version
  4. 4The version history and the cycle's own authority limit

Goal: treat promotion and rollback as planned steps: promote with a written way back, and roll back by the trigger, with no hunt for someone to blame.

02 · Four rules for promoting and going back

G2 put the process under version control and G3 promoted autonomy actions. Here the version is that of the agent package (prompt, context, model, tools) that came out of a LOOP-R cycle.

1. The promotion carries the rollback plan

Carlos approved v2.2 in week 7 with the rollback plan already written: a watch window of two weeks; a trigger of missed delivery-date promises above 3.4% in a week, or one critical error; who decides, Patrícia; how to go back, Rafael points Hora to v2.1 within 2 hours.

Deciding the rollback after the problem hits is deciding in a panic, and it almost always turns into an argument about blame.

RulePromoting needs approval from someone who did not propose it. Rolling back by the written trigger is the duty of whoever operates it, and it does not wait for a meeting.

2. The trigger allows for chance

Missed promises are a rare event: across 160 delivery-date conversations a week, the rate moves on its own. The trigger adds two standard errors for the week to the pilot rate (1.5%): 2 × √(0.015 × 0.985 ÷ 160) ≈ 1.9 points. Limit: 3.4%.

So a week with 4 missed promises (2.5%) does not roll back; a week with 7 (4.4%) does. It is the same calculation LOOP-R uses for tolerance.

CounterexampleIn the LOOP-R example, 6 complaints against 1, out of 300 per version, with zero tolerance, threw out a good idea by chance.

3. Roll back fast, with no one to blame

In week 8, logistics changed the delivery day for the Sorocaba route and the table was not updated. Missed promises: 7 of 158 (4.4%), above 3.4%. The conversations taken over by Juliana stayed at 19.0%: the target was doing fine, the guardrail fell.

Patrícia decided by the trigger; Juliana was told beforehand; Rafael pointed back to v2.1 within 2 hours; v2.2 was kept in storage. Carlos was informed with the number.

A cause, not someone to blameThe table had no expiry date and no notice for route changes. The fix is in the document (owner, expiry, notice), not in Valdir.

4. Version history and the cycle's authority limit

Each version gets one line in the history: what changed, why, who approved it, the status. The version that was rolled back is not erased; it becomes a memory of what did not work and why.

The cycle itself has an authority limit. In LOOP-R, L0 only proposes; L1 promotes with one person's approval for each change; L2 promotes on its own, and only after at least five promotions at L1 with no rollbacks. Horizonte is at L1.

Next stepv2.3: the same table, with Valdir as owner, a weekly expiry date, and a notice for route changes. It goes back through the 30 cases and a new pilot.

2. Whether the limit is so tight that normal variation sets it off, or so

Cycle authority limit: L1. No row deleted.

  • Promoting without a trigger. When the problem hits, nobody knows whether to roll back.
  • A trigger with no math. Too tight, and it rolls back by chance; too wide, and it never fires.
  • Waiting for a meeting to roll back. Rolling back by the written trigger needs no approval.
  • Deleting the version that failed. It is the memory of what did not work.
  • Looking for someone to blame. The question is what changed and what protection was missing.

    05 · Practice

    Horizonte's logs are for practice; the promotion and the rehearsed rollback of your agent are for real.

    1 · Hora's promotion, rollback, and history

    30 min

    Using the numbers from this lesson, fill in the promotion log for v2.2 (week 7) and the rollback log for week 8. Redo the trigger calculation. Build Hora's history with versions 2.0 to 2.3 and mark the cycle's authority limit.

    Done when: the promotion has an approver different from whoever proposed it, a watch window, a trigger with a number and the calculation (1.5% + 1.9 = 3.4%), who decides the rollback, the steps with times, and the people notified; the rollback has the number that fired it (7 of 158), the probable cause, and the next candidate; and the history has 4 lines, none erased.

    2 · Your agent's rehearsed rollback

    45 min

    For the change in your lesson 5 cycle (or for your agent's next change), write the promotion log with the calculated trigger. Then rehearse the rollback in a test environment or outside business hours: point to the previous version, time it, and check that nothing was lost.

    Done when: the promotion log is saved with the trigger as a number and the calculation; the rollback was rehearsed once with the time measured; your agent's version history exists; and whoever decides the rollback confirmed in writing that they know this.

    07 · Summary

    Promotion with a way backWatch window, trigger as a number, who decides, and how to roll back, written beforehand.
    Trigger with a numberThe pilot rate plus two standard errors for the week: it neither rolls back by chance nor never rolls back.
    Roll back with no one to blameKnown version, warning first, nothing erased; then the cause and a safeguard.
    History and authority limitEvery version is logged; the cycle moves from L1 to L2 only with evidence.
